# ArtScience Museum model (SG-048)

## Design and references

Replace the generic OpenStreetMap extrusions of the ArtScience Museum (10 Bayfront Avenue, Marina Bay Sands; Safdie Architects / Moshe Safdie; opened 17 February 2011; checklist task **SG-048**) with a procedural landmark model in the existing landmark kit: the "welcoming hand" / lotus. A round bowl is lifted over its lily pond on a core and opens into ten broad petals of spheroid form with blunt, cut ends. They rise in a progression around the centre, from the short western petals to the 60 m northern one, and a concave dish with an oculus lies between them.

- [Marina Bay Sands owner factsheet (PDF)](https://www.marinabaysands.com/content/dam/singapore/marinabaysands/master/main/home/company-information/media-centre/factsheets/amenities/artscience-museum.pdf) — designed by Moshe Safdie; the building is composed of 10 "fingers", **the tallest standing at 60 metres above ground**. This is the model's documented height.
- [Wikipedia: ArtScience Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArtScience_Museum) — a round base in the middle with ten extensions ("fingers") in the shape of a flower; **skylights at the fingertips**; rainwater is channelled through the **bowl-shaped roof into a reflecting pond** at the building's lowest level.
- [e-architect: Marina Bay Sands](https://www.e-architect.com/singapore/marina-bay-sands) (Safdie Architects' press text) — a base surrounded by a **giant lily pond**, and a flower of **10 petals generated by the geometry of spheroids of varying radii** that seemingly floats above it; the petals progress in size and height around the centre.
- [OpenStreetMap relation 14314416](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/14314416) — the mapped building, its outline and its 60-plus `building:part` slices (see below).

## Approach and scope

True bearings come from `kit.compass`; every ring is built about the round base's centre.

- **Petals**: each petal is a stack of `kit.loft`s through horizontal slices of its profile in (reach, height). The outer surface is a cubic curve from the bowl's underside (11.2 m out, 12.6 m up) to the outer lip, swelling and turning to the slope the photographs show at the end. The cut end runs straight from the lip to the end's top. The inner surface is a cubic curve from the top back down to the dish rim. Below 21 m each petal fills its share of the circle between its neighbours' bearings, so together they close into the round bowl. Above 21 m each narrows toward its end width, leaving the notches between petals. Backs and sides are stainless grey, upper faces and the cut ends' frames white. The western and south-eastern ends carry a glazed window band. The near-level ends of the two tallest petals are glazed whole: the fingertip skylights.
- **Dish**: the petals' upper faces fall to a rim 9 m out at 29.5 m, then to the dark oculus shaft.
- **Under the bowl**: a glazed core, a white V truss round it, the seven dark columns at their mapped positions leaning out to the bowl, two white stair towers, and the glazed entrance pavilion on the north-east toward the Helix Bridge.
- **Pond**: a shallow lily pond round the base, widest on the south-west, with a scatter of pads; paved where the entrance meets it.
- **Collision**: the core, columns' feet, stair towers, entrance and pond collide as ground parts. The bowl is filled from its underside to where the petals part, and each petal in three height bands over its plan in that band. That leaves the space under the bowl, the dish and the notches between the western petals open.
- No sign is modelled: the photographs show no lettering on the petals (identity signage is at entrance scale). Interiors, the rainwater waterfall through the oculus, the dish's raised rim and the petals' twist and steel panel seams are simplified to what reads at map distance.

## Verification

- `tests/landmark-artscience-museum.test.cjs` builds the model on the real generated anchor and probes it from independently located lon/lat points. It checks that exactly relation 14314416 and its 63 member ways are replaced, and that the pavilions, the Shoppes slices (SG-047), the Crystal Pavilion (SG-057) and the Apple dome (SG-049) remain in the registration. It checks ten petals: the northern one at 60 m, falling clockwise through the north-east and south-east, the five western ones alike at 33–37 m. Each petal reaches within 5 m of the mapped outline on its bearing and keeps to its own bearing. The bowl's underside is 12.6 m up with nothing under its western flank but the pond, the entrance pavilion stands on the north-east, the lily pond has its pads, and there is no sign. It also checks finite geometry and zero coplanar flicker. Collision is solid in the core, bowl, tallest and long south-eastern petals and the entrance, and open under the bowl, under the south-eastern petal, in the dish, in a western notch and in the bay. **9,370 triangles in 11 material batches** (budget 65,000/16). The model is also in the generic documented-height loop in `tests/landmark-models.test.cjs` at 60 m.
- Rendered checks (headless Chrome, WebGL): the [four-view Tiny overview](images/artscience-museum-overview.png), and perspective views matched to the photographs above (from the north-north-west, the east-north-east, the west and the east-south-east) in the Tiny and day looks. Each matched view shows the same petal at the same place as its photograph: the northern crescent, the north-eastern petal facing the Helix Bridge, the long south-eastern petals and the five western ends with windows. The loaded city view (`/?look=tiny&at=artscience-museum&camera=map`) shows the lotus on the Bayfront point inside its promenade, with no generic museum mass left under it.
- Known limitations: the western heights, end widths and profile curvature are estimates as listed above; the petals have no twist, so the real building's pinwheel overlap reads only in plan; the dish is a plain cone with no raised rim; the interior and waterfall are not modelled.
