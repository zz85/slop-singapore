Program: `js/landmark-models/ocbc-centre.js`; tests: `tests/landmark-ocbc-centre.test.cjs`; OCBC's mark: `ocbc` in `js/landmark-logos.js`. Built on 3 November 2026 with the landmark kit and the city's normal/Tiny material pairing, replacing a placeholder that extruded the mapped parts.

## The buildings

OCBC Centre, 65 Chulia Street, was designed by I. M. Pei & Partners with BEP Akitek, with Ove Arup & Partners as engineers. It opened on 1 October 1976. It is 52 storeys and 197.7 m, and was Southeast Asia's tallest building when it opened. Its nickname is "the calculator". Two later blocks share the site: OCBC Centre South, 18 Church Street (seven storeys, 1985), and OCBC Centre East, 63 Chulia Street (1996).

What the sources and photographs establish:

- **Structure.** Two semicircular reinforced-concrete cores carry the tower. They hold the lifts, stairs and services, and they form its rounded ends. Three post-tensioned, concrete-encased transfer girders span between the cores at the 4th, 20th and 35th floors. Each girder carries 14 floors of column-free offices, so the three sections could be built at the same time (docomomo, Dominik Gehl). State of Buildings adds that the floors reach up to six metres out from each core.
- **What that looks like.** On both long faces, each section of offices is a box that cantilevers out beyond the cores' faces. The boxes have grey ribbon windows in white frames between light grey spandrels, eleven windows across. Each box's top is a light band. The top box's last storey is paler aqua glass. Each box's foot is a light fascia over a dark recessed edge with the cantilever brackets' ends: the heavier line that marks every section from a distance. Between the boxes the girder floors return to the cores' plane in plain granite. Those recesses are where the peregrine falcons nest: 37 floors up, NParks, November 2024. The cores and the girder floors are clad in warm grey granite tiles.
- **Crown.** Above the top box, the cores and a plain wall about 8.6 m high rise to a flat roof. From above, the roof is a light stadium round a darker sunken plant well. Nothing stands above the roofline in any photograph.
- **Sign.** OCBC's red mark and the name "OCBC" (formerly "OCBC Bank") hang on the crown wall just under the roofline. They appear twice, point-symmetrically:
  - on the face to the river (north-north-east), starting just inside the top box's east end and running about 15 m west;
  - on the face to Chinatown (south-south-west), at the west end.

  Photographs from 2006 and 2015 show "OCBC Bank". Both August 2023 photographs show "OCBC" alone. A Singapore flag panel beside the river-side mark in August 2023 was a National Day decoration and is left off.
- **Foot.** The cores reach the ground. Between them is the banking hall (about 1,200 m² and 12 m high, behind a transparent façade, per docomomo), with a plain granite band above it up to the lowest box. Along Chulia Street that box overhangs the walk 16 m up; the OCBCBase001 photograph shows its soffit over a recessed wall and entrance. The Henry Moore *Large Reclining Figure* stands on the plaza at the west core (OSM node 8336481777, outside this site).
- **Podium.** South-west of the tower is a six-level car park (OSM 407705497) whose north-east edge lies on the stacks' line. The 2015 view from the Stamford shows cars parked on its roof. The aerial shows a block of cooling-tower cells and a round tank at its north-west end. Its service side along the alley to the Yueh Hai Ching Temple is grey concrete with roller shutters.
- **Centre South.** A long block with two rounded ends (OSM 407705501). The 2015 Stamford view shows its rounded west end in light horizontal bands and dark ribbon windows. The alley photograph shows square granite piers along its north side, with the ground floor set back behind them.
- **Centre East.** Beige granite with deep punched windows (Commons, 2006). Toward the tower's east core it turns a curved front of banded grey-green glass (Penang Travel Tips; the 2023 river photograph), with a beige crown above the glass. The walk between the east core and this curve is about 6 m wide.
- **Plans.** OCBC is exploring a redevelopment of 63 and 65 Chulia Street and 18 Church Street (April 2024). Later reports say the plans are on the back burner. Nothing has been built or demolished, so the model shows the buildings as they stand.

## Frame and drawing

The program works in the city frame about the anchor. The slab's four straight-face corners come from the points the tower ring shares with the two "Lower Block" strips. Their mean is the slab's middle.

- `u` runs along bearing 116°, toward the east core.
- `v` runs along bearing 26°, toward Chulia Street and the river.
- The straight middle is 2 × 19.1 m long. The slab is 2 × 11.2 m across, which is also the cores' radius, so the cores are drawn as true half-circles of 16 sides.
- The stacks cover the straight middle and reach 5.5 m out. The mapped strips are 4.8–6.3 m wide; the published figure is "up to six metres".

Centre East's curved front is the mapped run from its north end's west corner round to the podium's notch, found by the mapped points nearest two positions in `(u, v)` and rounded by corner cutting. The rest of its ring is as mapped. The podium's plant is placed from its long edge on the stacks' line.

## Collision

Explicit collision volumes:

- the slab and cores from 12 m to the roof (the middle exactly, the half-circles strip by strip);
- each stack, from its soffit to its band;
- Centre South above its colonnade;
- the podium and Centre East, filled so that they can be landed on.

Ground parts collide automatically: the cores' feet, the banking hall, the podium link, Centre South's ground floor and piers, and the walk paving (0.32 m).

These stay open to walk and fly through:

- the walk under the river-side stack, to 15.9 m;
- the recessed girder floors in front of the cores' faces;
- the walk between the east core and Centre East;
- the ground just beyond the cores' ends.

## Limitations

- **Heights.** The stacks and gaps are measured from one perspective-corrected photograph at an assumed lowest soffit of 16 m. That assumption makes the published 198 m and 52 storeys agree at a 3.656 m storey, and the 2015 view from above confirms the crown. The OSM "Lower/Middle/Upper Block" heights (16–76, 85–136, 142–188 m) disagree by up to 8 m and are not used. The exact floor numbers of the girder floors (4, 20, 35) are not reconciled with the 13 window rows plus fascia seen on each box.
- **Plan.** The straight middle and the width are as mapped. The roof seen from above is about 60 m long, matching the half-circle ends rather than the mapped ring's 65 m. The stacks are given the mapped strip's average depth, and the boxes' exact end positions are estimated.
- **Base.** The banking hall's front is modelled as plain glass between the cores on both faces, with a granite band above. On the Chinatown side the strip under the stack is filled as part of the podium, as the site outline has it; no photograph of that side's ground floor was found. The entrance portal at the west core, the lettering "OCBC Centre 65 Chulia Street", the ground-floor "OCBC Bank" signs and the glass canopy to Centre East are not modelled.
- **Signs.** Their position and the mark's shape come from the 2023 photographs and a 2006 close-up. No photograph from 2024–2026 was found, so the current lettering is assumed unchanged from August 2023. The 2015 photograph showed a small red "OCBC Bank" on the girder band over the banking hall; it is left off because no current photograph confirms it.
- **Neighbours.** Centre South's height (28 m) and its colonnade all round, the podium's 15.5 m, and Centre East's 16 glass storeys and beige crown are estimated from distant views. The cores' fine granite tile grid is not drawn.
