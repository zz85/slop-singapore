# MAS Building model

Checklist **SG-090**. Program: `js/landmark-models/mas-building.js`; tests: `tests/landmark-mas-building.test.cjs`. Built on 4 November 2026 with the landmark kit and the city's normal/Tiny material pairing. It replaces a placeholder that extruded the mapped parts to 100 m.

The MAS Building at 10 Shenton Way is the headquarters of the Monetary Authority of Singapore. RSP Architects Planners & Engineers designed it; it was built from 1981 to 1985 and refurbished in 2005. MAS moved in from the SIA Building in 1985. It stands on the block between Shenton Way (south-east), Prince Edward Road (north-east), Springleaf Tower and Anson Road (north-west) and Parsi Road (south-west), opposite the Singapore Conference Hall and the Shenton Way bus terminal.

The tower is a sheer slab of terracotta tiles, 46 by 66 m and 138 m tall. Its two kinds of face:

- The **short faces** (Prince Edward Road, north-east; Parsi Road, south-west) are frames. Tiled end frames and a middle group of four piers stand round bays of close vertical louvres. Dark slots, window slots and a set-back panel sit in the middle group. Under the crown run three horizontal slats with dark gaps between them.
- The **long faces** (Springleaf Tower, north-west; Shenton Way, south-east) are deep louvred recesses. Each runs from the Parsi Road face's end frame to a solid tiled end beside the Prince Edward Road face. The crown band is carried over each recess on a lattice, with the top storey's glass behind it.

A 9 m crown band runs round all four faces. A four-storey podium of polished brown granite with rounded corners wraps the tower's foot on Parsi Road and Shenton Way. The porte-cochère over the drop-off toward Prince Edward Road carries the MAS crest and name.

### Which face is which

The photographs show two kinds of face but not, directly, which is which. The plan follows from three of them:

- **Lannuzel 2009** (the camera to the north-north-east): the frame face on the left is the short north-east face; the recess face on the right, with Springleaf Tower beyond, is the long north-west face.
- **Tye's square-on photograph of 2017** shows a frame face whose middle group takes 46 % of its width and whose bays take about 18 % each. That matches the 46 m north-east face as Lannuzel shows it. A recess face with a projecting crown stands to its left: seen from the north-east, that is the south-east (Shenton Way) face. The piers' shadows fall to their right, so the sun was behind the camera's left, in the east-north-east. That is a July morning.
- **Tye's wide-angle photograph of the same day** has both faces in sun: the recess face on the left and the frame face on the right. In late July that is possible only for a camera east of the tower in the morning (sun about 70°), with the Shenton Way and Prince Edward Road faces both lit; seen from the south, the two faces could not both be lit. Beyond the podium's rounded end it shows a granite block on round columns with the MAS sign. That is the porte-cochère in the outline's north-east wing.

So the long faces are the recesses and the short ones the frames. In Rafi's 2023 photograph up the south corner, the Shenton Way recess' louvres run to the corner and its solid end is far off. That agrees with the solid ends standing beside the Prince Edward Road face, as Lannuzel shows on the north-west.

### Height

**The model is 138 m to the parapet, as Penang Travel Tips gives it. CTBUH's 103.8 m (and OSM's 102 m, likely taken from it) does not fit the building.** Three checks support 138 m:

- **The square-on photograph (Tye 2017).** The north-east face is 226 px wide (46 m, 4.9 px/m). The parapet is at row 55, and the horizon, at the roofs of the cars near the camera, is about row 725. That gives 130–137 m.
- **The aerial lean.** In the same aerial tiles the roof is displaced from its mapped base by 143 px along the lean. Springleaf Tower's crown ring (165 m) is displaced 175 px and International Plaza's roof (190 m) 186 px. That puts MAS at 135–146 m.
- **Lannuzel 2009.** In this perspective-corrected photograph, the roof corners and the base fit a level camera only with the horizon near the cars' roofs. That gives 132–152 m. A 104 m tower would need the horizon 200 px higher.

The 30 storeys both sources give are taken as office floors over a tall ground storey under a crown of plant: 138 m / 30 is about 4.6 m a floor overall. OSM's 37 levels look copied from Springleaf Tower's tags.

## Signs

One sign, as Tye photographed it in 2017: the MAS crest (a gold medallion on a tall navy panel) and "Monetary Authority / of Singapore" in gold. It hangs with `kit.hang` on the porte-cochère's side toward Shenton Way, its top 13.9 m up, 5.5 m wide.

The crest is `LOGOS.mas` in `js/landmark-logos.js`, simplified from that photograph. No drawing of it is published on Commons, and its figure is too small in the photograph to draw. No photograph shows a name or mark on the tower itself, so none is modelled there.

## Limitations

- **The height (138 m) rests on one published figure** and on the photographic and aerial checks above; the CTBUH figure is 103.8 m. The crown, slat and podium heights are estimates from photographs.
- **Which faces are recesses is inferred** from the sun's direction and the faces' proportions in photographs (see *Which face is which*); no drawing or plan was found.
- The recesses' solid ends (16 m) are estimates: every photograph sees them foreshortened from the corner they belong to.
- The frame faces' middle group is drawn symmetric. In Lannuzel 2009 its two halves differ slightly: a pair of piers on one side of the panel, pier–slot–pier on the other.
- The louvres are painted (the kit's strips pattern), not modelled fin by fin. The real fins stand proud and cast shadows.
- The lattice is drawn as posts on a chord. The real one is a deeper truss with diagonal members, seen against the sky.
- Estimated because no photograph shows the north-west and north-east sides at ground level, nor the podium's Parsi Road face whole:
  - the porte-cochère's height and columns;
  - the annex's height;
  - the portal's place;
  - the drive's soffit.
  The covered drive is placed where OSM maps it (way 47345218).
- The roof plant's layout is simplified from the aerial.
- Left out: the flagpole, the trees round the site, the podium's interior banking hall and the basement car park's ramps.
