# Central Fire Station (SG-253)

This model covers the 1908–09 Central Fire Station at 62 Hill Street — Singapore's oldest surviving fire station, a national monument still in operational use — in its present (2025–26) outward form: red-brick ranges around an open drill yard, the striped hose/lookout tower, and the domed corner turrets.

## References

- [National Heritage Board's Roots entry](https://www.roots.gov.sg/places/places-landing/Places/national-monuments/central-fire-station) gives the site (62 Hill Street, at the foot of Fort Canning Hill), construction from 1908 to completion in 1909, designer William Ferguson, and the "blood-and-bandage" reading of the façade: exposed red brick ("blood") with white-painted plaster bands ("bandage"). The National Library Board's [Infopedia article](https://eresources.nlb.gov.sg/infopedia/articles/SIP_857_2004-12-15.html) adds that the three-storey main building held the engine house, firemen's quarters, workshops and a training yard, and that it was gazetted a national monument on 18 December 1998.
- The NHB's republication at [Wonderwall](https://wonderwall.sg/places/national-monuments-of-singapore-central-fire-station) describes the 34-metre lookout tower, once manned around the clock; the model takes the tower (to its cupola finial) as its 34 m datum.
- Reference photographs viewed 4 November 2026 on Wikimedia Commons: [Central Fire Station, Singapore, Jan 06](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Central_Fire_Station,_Singapore,_Jan_06.JPG) (Hill Street front: six arched red engine doors between fine red/white striped piers, shuttered veranda upper floors, white parapet, the striped tower behind, plainer brick side wing to the south-east, domed turrets at the north-west end), [CentralFireStation-Singapore-20081107-full](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:CentralFireStation-Singapore-20081107-full.jpg) (tower detail: white quoin stripes on brick, slit window, louvred stage with small white columns, white attic stage with an arched window, pale green-copper pyramidal roof and domed lantern cupola), and [Central Fire Station, October 2025](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Central_Fire_Station,_October_2025.jpg) (current condition from Coleman Street: shuttered windows, white floor bands, the round-window turret).

## Footprint and massing

The 25 October 2026 local OSM extract maps the whole complex as one 41-point way, [way 172368086](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/172368086) (`building=fire_station`, `building:levels=3`, `height=20`, `start_date=1908`). This single way is the only replacement; there are no relations or inner rings, and the drill yard is open because the mapped ring itself is only the perimeter ranges (about 1,670 m² within an 84 × 59 m bounding span). The Armenian Church, Children's Museum and National Archives across the surrounding streets remain mapped.

The model uses a local drawing frame from the ring's south-west (Hill Street) corner at 103.8485982°E, 1.2921487°N: `a` metres south-east along Hill Street (true bearing 117°) and `b` metres north-east into the site (bearing 27°). In that frame the ring decomposes into the ranges the model builds, abutting rather than overlapping:

- Hill Street front range `a 0–55, b 0–11.8` — ground floor (to 4.8 m) in punched brick with six red engine-door panels and fine striped piers on the street face (south-east section, doors centred `a 33–50`), shuttered upper floors to 12.6 m, white parapet to 13.4 m.
- Coleman Street (north-west) wing `a 0.6–12.8, b 11.8–32.2`, same roofline, with the twin domed turrets (white stages to 16.4 m, dark domes to about 19 m) at its street end.
- South-east side wing `a 65.4–71.1, b 13–32` and its street pavilion `a 57.8–69, b 4.4–12.5` (lower, to 10.5 m), following the ring's stepped south-east end.
- Rear (north-east) barracks wing `a 53.3–65.4, b 32–60.5`, same 13.4 m roofline.
- Hose/lookout tower centred `a 54.5, b 15`, where the ring widens behind the front range: a 7 × 7 m brick shaft with white corner quoin stripes rising from the front roof to 23 m, a white cornice and attic stage to 28 m, a pale copper-green pyramidal roof to 30.8 m and the domed lantern cupola to 34 m.

White floor bands (0.55 m deep, standing 0.12 m proud) run around every range at the floor lines, giving the "bandage" stripes at map scale. The mapped `height=20` describes the whole complex loosely; the ranges' 13.4 m and the tower stages are visual estimates from the photographs against the documented 34 m tower total (three 3.6–4.8 m storeys for the ranges). The drill yard (`a 13–49, b 12–60`) and the ground below the tower's roof stages stay open in geometry and collision; small rear steps in the mapped ring's south-east edge (ring points 28–38) are absorbed into the side wing and street pavilion rectangles.

- [Four-view preview](images/central-fire-station-overview.png) and [city view](images/central-fire-station-city.jpg). With the repo served locally, use `/tools/landmark-preview.html?model=central-fire-station&view=s` or `/?look=tiny&at=central-fire-station&camera=map`.

Simplifications at miniature scale: the engine doors are flat red panels with white lintels between striped piers rather than real arches; the north-west block's ornate porch, pediments and the tower attic's arched window are suggested by the façade pattern and massing, not modelled piece by piece; the pyramid roof's collision is a conservative inset volume inside the visible slope. Estimated heights and tower stage divisions follow the photographs against the documented 34 m total, as noted above.
