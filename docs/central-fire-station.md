# Central Fire Station (SG-253)

This model covers the 1908–09 Central Fire Station at 62 Hill Street — Singapore's oldest surviving fire station, a national monument still in operational use — in its present (2023–25) outward form: red-brick ranges around an open drill yard, the street front facing Hill Street with its white pedimented frontispiece and twin domed turrets, and the striped hose tower behind them.

## References

- [National Heritage Board's Roots entry](https://www.roots.gov.sg/places/places-landing/Places/national-monuments/central-fire-station) gives the site (62 Hill Street, at the foot of Fort Canning Hill), construction from 1908 to completion in 1909, designer William Ferguson, and the "blood-and-bandage" reading of the façade: exposed red brick ("blood") with white-painted plaster bands ("bandage"). The National Library Board's [Infopedia article](https://eresources.nlb.gov.sg/infopedia/articles/SIP_857_2004-12-15.html) adds that the three-storey main building held the engine house, firemen's quarters, workshops and a training yard, and that it was gazetted a national monument on 18 December 1998.
- The NHB's republication at [Wonderwall](https://wonderwall.sg/places/national-monuments-of-singapore-central-fire-station) describes the 34-metre lookout tower, once manned around the clock; the model takes the tower (to its cupola finial) as its 34 m datum.
- Reference photographs viewed 4 November 2026 on Wikimedia Commons: [Estación central de bomberos, Singapur, 2023-08-18](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Estaci%C3%B3n_central_de_bomberos,_Singapur,_2023-08-18,_DD_10.jpg) (the Hill Street front square-on: from the south, a plain brick end, the 1909 block with seven red arched doors under a red balcony, its white frontispiece in the middle rising to a pediment between two white turrets with pale green copper domes and spikes, the tower centred behind them, then the lower extension with six red doors under white shutters, white balustrades with pinnacles, and a plain brick block at the north end), [At Singapore 2023 045 - Central Fire Station](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:At_Singapore_2023_045_-_Central_Fire_Station.jpg) (the same front from the north along Hill Street, the tower rising behind the frontispiece), [2016 Singapur, Museum Planning Area, Budynek główny straży pożarnej (01)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:2016_Singapur,_Museum_Planning_Area,_Budynek_g%C5%82%C3%B3wny_stra%C5%BCy_po%C5%BCarnej_(01).jpg) (from the south: the plain brick south end, the tower behind it), [CentralFireStation-Singapore-20081107-full](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:CentralFireStation-Singapore-20081107-full.jpg) (tower detail: white quoin stripes on brick, slit window, louvred stage with small white columns, white attic stage with an arched window, pale green-copper pyramidal roof and domed lantern cupola), [Central Fire Station, Singapore, Jan 06](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Central_Fire_Station,_Singapore,_Jan_06.JPG) (2006, from the north: the turrets' domes were then dark grey) and [Central Fire Station, October 2025](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Central_Fire_Station,_October_2025.jpg) (from the south: the domes are pale green copper today).

## Footprint and massing

The 25 October 2026 local OSM extract maps the whole complex as one 41-point way, [way 172368086](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/172368086) (`building=fire_station`, `building:levels=3`, `height=20`, `start_date=1908`). This single way is the only replacement; there are no relations or inner rings, and the drill yard is open because the mapped ring itself is only the perimeter ranges (about 1,670 m² within an 84 × 59 m bounding span). The Armenian Church, Children's Museum and National Archives across the surrounding streets remain mapped.

The model uses a local drawing frame from the ring's south-west corner at 103.8485982°E, 1.2921487°N: `a` metres along the long south-west range (true bearing 117°) and `b` metres north-north-east (bearing 27°), parallel to Hill Street, which runs along the ring's east side at about `a 74–78` (OSM's Hill Street nodes). In that frame the ring decomposes into the ranges the model builds, abutting rather than overlapping:

- The plain back range `a 0–54.5, b 0–11.8`, its brick face toward Fort Canning, and the north-west block `a 0.6–12.8, b 11.8–32.2` across the drill yard: three storeys to 12.6 m.
- The 1909 block along Hill Street, `a 53.3–69, b 12.5–32`, narrower at its south end (`a 57.8–69, b 4.4–12.5`), with its yard-side court `a 48.7–53.3, b 11.8–32.1`, to 12.6 m; the small porch `a 61.5–64.7, b −0.9–4.4` at the south end, to 9.9 m.
- The lower extension north along Hill Street, `a 53.3–65.4, b 32–60.5`, to 10.9 m, its face set back to `a 65.4`.
- The street front. The 1909 block's face at `a 69` and its white frontispiece projecting to `a 71.1` between `b 13.1` and `23.4` carry seven red arched doors (two, three on the frontispiece, two) between red-and-white striped piers, the red balcony at 5–6 m, white plaster upper floors with striped piers, and pinnacles on the parapet; the frontispiece rises to 15.8 m under a pediment to 17.4 m. The extension carries six red doors at 4.1 m centres, white shutters between its red piers, and pinnacles. Two turrets (2.8 m white stages from the roof to 19.4 m, cornice, drum, pale green copper domes to 21.9 m, spikes to 24 m) stand on the frontispiece's corners at `b 14.5` and `22.0`.
- The hose/lookout tower centred behind the frontispiece at `a 54.5, b 18.25`: a 7 × 7 m brick shaft with white corner quoin stripes rising from the roof to 20.75 m, a louvred stage, a white cornice and attic stage to 28 m, a pale copper-green pyramidal roof to 30.8 m and the domed lantern cupola to 34 m.

White floor bands (0.55 m deep, standing proud) run around every range at the floor lines, giving the "bandage" stripes at map scale. The mapped `height=20` describes the whole complex loosely; the ranges' heights, the frontispiece, turret and tower stages are visual estimates from the square-on 2023 photograph (about 19 px per metre at the façade) against the documented 34 m tower total. The drill yard (`a 13–49, b 12–60`) stays open in geometry and collision; small steps in the mapped ring are absorbed into the range rectangles.

A first version of this model (PR #29) read the ring the wrong way round: it put the engine doors, striped piers and veranda on the south-west range facing Fort Canning, left the Hill Street ranges plain, placed the turrets with dark domes on the far north-west block, and the tower 2.75 m south of its place behind the frontispiece. It was corrected against the photographs and the aerial on 4 November 2026.

## Verification

- The tests check the single replacement ID; probe each range's height and the open yard at coordinates placed from the drawing frame above; check that OSM's own Hill Street nodes lie along `a 73–79` with nothing built on them, that all thirteen red doors face Hill Street, that the pediment, both domed turrets and the tower stand in line with the turrets on the street front and the tower centred behind them, and that the back range toward Fort Canning has no street front; and check collision through the ranges, frontispiece, tower shaft and pyramid roof while the yard, Hill Street and the slope toward Fort Canning stay clear. The orientation test fails on the first version.
- The full Windows suite passes 637 of 638 checks; the one failure is the known tile-worker fixture, which fails identically on `main`.
- The preview uses 3,348 triangles, 279 parts and 13 material batches, and has zero coplanar flicker pairs. Perspective renders from the photographs' bearings (square-on to Hill Street, from the north along it) were compared side by side with the photographs in the daylight look, the four views checked in the Tiny look, and the loaded city checked from the map camera in WebGPU (the front facing Hill Street, the yard open, the neighbours undisturbed).
- [Four-view preview](images/central-fire-station-overview.png) and [city view](images/central-fire-station-city.jpg). With the repo served locally, use `/tools/landmark-preview.html?model=central-fire-station&view=e` or `/?look=tiny&at=central-fire-station&camera=map`.

Simplifications at miniature scale: the arched doors are flat red panels with white lintels; the striped piers are white bands on red; the balconies' railings, the frontispiece's arched windows and roundel, the turrets' open arcades and the tower attic's arched windows are suggested by façade patterns and massing, not modelled piece by piece; the domes are eight-sided lofts; the pyramid roof's collision is a conservative inset volume inside the visible slope. The back range's and north-west block's outer faces are not documented in the photographs and are drawn plain.
