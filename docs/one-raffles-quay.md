## The building

One Raffles Quay, 1 Raffles Quay, was completed in 2006 for Hongkong Land, Keppel Land and Cheung Kong. Kohn Pedersen Fox was the design architect and Architects 61 the architect of record. Heintges designed the unitised curtain wall, Meinhardt the structure and services, and Obayashi built it. The site is a block between Raffles Quay (west), Marina Boulevard (north) and Marina View Link (east), with Central Boulevard's tunnel to the south.

What the sources and photographs establish:

- **Two towers.** The North Tower has 50 storeys and is 245 m high. The South Tower has 29 storeys and is 139.9 m high. KPF's text says the North Tower "straddles the MRT's subterranean tunnels and is rotated to meet the required tunnel clearances". In the map it is turned about 26° off the street grid, while the South Tower keeps to the grid.
- **North Tower's front blade and lantern.** Toward Collyer Quay and Raffles Place, the North Tower's front is a blade 5.7 m deep. Its two ends stand proud of the side faces in narrow recessed slots, which are the notches in the mapped outline. KPF: "A lantern crowns the front facade at the top." KPF's dusk aerial and the 2026 Commons photograph show the blade rising about two storeys past the side faces' parapet as a lit glass screen over a space frame. The Esri aerial shows a louvred band along its top.
- **North Tower's base.** KPF describes "an eight-storey lobby" toward Raffles Place. Behind its glass, a white raking member crosses the face to Raffles Quay, seen from the street (Heintges' elevation) and inside the lobby (KPF). At dusk the ground floor reads as a recessed band of warm glass under the lobby.
- **South Tower.** Its front blade to Raffles Quay is about 3 m deep, with a slot at its south-west corner (the 2006 Commons photographs and Heintges' elevation). At the top the blade carries a lighter screen. Stone piers stand under the glass at the ground floor. On the south and east faces, the storeys to about 56 m have grey louvre bands between ribbons of glass (KPF's aerial; the 2009 shift-lens photograph).
- **Façade.** Both towers are wrapped in pale aqua-green glass between fine silver mullions every 1.5 m, with a hairline at each storey. Architects 61 describe "a glassy, transparent curtain wall with a slight greenish tint". In sun it reads pale aqua with sky reflections; in shade, a deeper sea green. There are no vertical fins and no chamfered corners. The crowns are flat, and each tower's top is set off by its lighter front blade.
- **Between the towers.** KPF: Tower 2 "is linked to Tower 1 by a District Cooling Plant". The plant is a block of staggered bronze louvres with light edges, under a glass band round its cooling towers. It stands on stone columns over the covered plaza and the drop-off. Architects 61 describe "a spacious covered plaza with bamboo landscaping and water features". The building's name is on a dark granite water trough on Raffles Quay (Heintges' elevation). OSM's 44 m tag on the site outline is the plant's height. The Esri aerial shows its six cooling cells between the towers.
- **Corner blocks.** A grey stone block with a sloping glass roof and a dark louvred panel on its street face fills the corner of Marina Boulevard and Marina View Link (the 2012 dusk photograph; KPF's aerial). A stone podium with a louvred panel wraps the South Tower's south and east sides, along Central Boulevard.
- **Signs.**
  - Deutsche Bank's name and mark, in blue, are on the South Tower's south podium face. The Commons photograph of 11 July 2026 shows them there, and Deutsche Bank's Singapore page (read 3 November 2026) gives its Asia Pacific head office as One Raffles Quay South Tower, Level 17.
  - Deutsche Bank and QBE plates stand over the South Tower's ground floor on Raffles Quay (Heintges' elevation). They are too small for the map and are not modelled.
  - No tenant sign hangs on either crown in any photograph from 2006 to 2026.

## Frame and drawing

The program works in the city frame about the anchor (x toward 121.8°, the street grid's east; z toward 211.8°). Every corner comes from a mapped ring, as the nearest mapped point to a position given in that frame. The program throws if none lies within 3 m. These corners are the North Tower's eight (the blade's west and north corners, the two slots' inner corners and ends, and the east and south corners) and the South Tower's four. The street edges are read from the outline where it crosses a line of the frame.

- **North Tower:** the blade (west corner, north corner, the two slots' inner corners) and the body behind it.
- **South Tower:** the mapped rectangle with a 1.2 × 1.2 m slot cut into its south face 3 m from the south-west corner. Its blade is the 3 m strip along its west face.
- **Cooling plant:** from 1.5 m behind the South Tower's face to Raffles Quay to a metre inside the outline's east edge, from the South Tower's north face to the North Tower's south corner.

- **Paving.** The site is paved to 0.32 m over the city's lawn. Six trees line the North Tower's forecourts on Raffles Quay and Marina Boulevard.

- **Deutsche Bank.** The name is 1.8 m high near the top of the podium's south face, with the slash-in-a-square mark to its right, both 0.3 m off the wall and under its 22 m top.

## Collision

- **Towers:** solid from their ground floors to their roof decks. Each blade's lantern is solid to its top, and the North Tower's body to its parapet's top.
- **Cooling plant:** solid from its 12 m soffit to 44 m.
- **Podium and stone block:** solid from the ground.
- **Canopies:** collide as thin slabs at 5.6 m.
- **Open:** the plaza and the drop-off under the plant are clear from the paving to 12 m, the forecourts are clear, and the air between the towers above the plant is clear to fly through.

## Limitations

- **Heights inside the towers.** The roof decks, parapets, lobby heights, the plant's soffit and the podium's and block's heights are estimated from photographs; no section was found. The South Tower's bands stop at an estimated 56 m (the car-park storeys' top).
- **Lantern.** It is a solid prism of lighter glass with a louvred top. The real one is a glass screen over an open space frame, lit at night. The South Tower's lighter crown screen is modelled the same way.
- **Raking member.** The North Tower's lobby shows one white raking member. The real lobby has several raking columns behind the glass (KPF's interior photograph); only the one seen from the street is modelled.
- **Plaza.** The water features, the escalators to the basement link and the arcade's interior are not modelled. The bamboo is a few green stems. The cooling plant's louvre pattern is random, not the real pattern.
- **Podium and stone block.** Their outlines are drawn from the site outline and the towers' corners, not from mapped ways (none exist). The podium's east side along Marina View Link and the drop-off's ramp under it are simplified.
- **Small signs.** The Deutsche Bank and QBE plates over the South Tower's ground floor are left out as too small for the map. The trough's lettering is a system font.
