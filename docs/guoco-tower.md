# Guoco Tower model

Checklist **SG-080**. Program: `js/landmark-models/guoco-tower.js`; tests: `tests/landmark-guoco-tower.test.cjs`. Rebuilt on 4 November 2026 with the landmark kit and the city's normal/Tiny material pairing. It replaces a stand-in of five plain prisms (56 triangles) that extruded the mapped parts in blue-grey glass.

Guoco Tower (Tanjong Pagar Centre, 1 Wallich Street) is GuocoLand's mixed-use development over Tanjong Pagar MRT station, completed in 2016: Singapore's tallest building. Skidmore, Owings & Merrill were the design architect, Architects 61 the local architect, Arup the structural and façade engineer, Meinhardt the MEP engineer, Samsung C&T the main contractor. The site holds:

- the 64-storey tower: Grade A offices on the lower floors, the 181 apartments of the Wallich Residence on levels 39–64;
- the free-standing 21-storey Sofitel Singapore City Centre;
- a six-storey podium of car parks, shops and restaurants;
- the Urban Park (more than 8,000 m²) with the City Room, a 3,000 m² public room under a 15 m solar-glass canopy.

### Heights

`info.height` is **283.7 m, CTBUH's architectural height**, which is also its height to tip. It is the top of the crown's sawtooth fins and screens. The 290 m that Wikipedia, SOM and GuocoLand quote is not used: it matches the 283.7 m plus the ground's 5–6 m above sea level, and the area's 280 m height control is set above sea level. The model is drawn from the ground, so it uses CTBUH's figure. The 268.3 m highest occupied floor sits under the 280 m roof.

The other heights were measured on SOM's east elevation (shift lens; the tower top at 283.7 m gives the scale, 2.56 px/m; the ground was located to ±15 px, about ±6 m, which shifts every height alike by up to 2 %):

- the residence's floors are 3.65 m apart: 31½ of them from the dark sky band to the top;
- the sky band at the residence's foot is at 167–172 m;
- the south block's roof is at about 168 m, its white pergola rising to 175.5 m;
- the seam between the slabs stops at about 166–168 m;
- the north strip steps back at 250–254 m;
- the sky frame's dark opening is at 261–275 m, three to four floors.

The hotel's 105.3 m is CTBUH's. The podium's 30 m is OSM's six levels; it agrees with the hotel's floors in SOM's photograph from the park.

- the tower T: u −18.8 to 42.9, v −4.0 to 48.9;
- the residence's south face vR at 23.3;
- the hotel: u −60.7 to −20.3, v 23.3 to 49.1;

## Palette

The colours come from the daylight photographs: Chainwit. 2025 and Bjoertvedt 2018 in midday sun, Beinling 2018 under a clear sky.

- The tower's glass is a deep blue, near navy in shadow; the sampled means are `#21374d`–`#536a80`, lighter where it reflects the sky.
- Its fins and ledges are fine silver lines, and its white elements (pergola, fins at the folds, sky-frame tray) are bright.
- The Sofitel's glass is a slightly lighter blue, `#33434e` sampled; its west wall is white, `#c6cacd` in shade.
- The podium's louvres are a mosaic of white and mid-grey.
- The City Room's canopy frame and columns are copper-brown, `#5c4d43` in shade; its solar glass is dark.

The Tiny look blends the panes 30 % toward the sky. The tower's glass is set dark and saturated so that it stays blue-grey there rather than going pastel.

## Signs

Only the two signs that the building carries high up today are modelled:

- **GuocoLand's red mark** near the top of the residence's south face, at its west end between the corner fin and the next. It is 5.6 m tall, its top 1 m under the 280 m roofline, drawn with `kit.sign` on the wall. It appears in every photograph from 2016 to 2025. The mark was added to `js/landmark-logos.js` as `guocoland`: three red bands stacked a little apart, their left ends rounding along one circle. It is simplified from the photographs, as no drawing is published on Commons.
- **SOFITEL** in gold capitals across the top of the hotel's south face, 4.4 m panel (letters about 2.5 m), hung with `kit.hang` on the crown screen (Bjoertvedt 2018; SOM's photograph from the park).

Left out:

- the "GUOCO TOWER" letters on the drop-off's low wall (a free-standing ground sign);
- the ING sign on the podium in SOM's 2017 photographs (the tenant could not be confirmed for 2026);
- the Sofitel's ground-floor entrance sign;
- the shop signs in the City Room.

## Limitations

- The tower's plan is OSM's two four-point boxes. The real faces taper slightly on every side (SOM); only the south face's lean, the clearest in the photographs, is modelled. The east and west faces are vertical.
- The split of the plan into the south block, the seam and the residence is measured on one photograph from the east, and the seam's set-back is estimated.
- Estimated from photographs, not surveyed:
  - every height except the top, the hotel's and the City Room's;
  - the lobby's 18 m;
  - the pergola's frame;
  - the balcony stacks' pattern (simplified to four columns with two breaks each);
  - the fins' lengths and the sawtooth's slope;
  - the sky frame.
- The City Room canopy is drawn as a rectangle as wide as the tower. Its north edge and the trees' grid are estimates, and the BIPV glass is plain dark glass. It lies outside the replaced ways, over the hidden stale outline 117052184.
- The residence's north face is drawn plain with ledges. The stepped dark openings on it in Jussun's 2016 photograph from Maxwell Road are left out.
- The kit has no see-through glass. The pergola, fins and canopy frame are solid beams, and the podium's louvres are a painted pattern.
- Left out:
  - the roof gardens and stairs over the MRT station, the MRT entrance pavilions and the Urban Park's planting (all the city's, outside the replaced ways);
  - the podium's terraces and openings;
  - the residence's rooftop plant;
  - the drop-off's sign wall.
